Inclusive Learning Design in Certificate III Hairdressing

Introduction
Inclusive learning design is part of vocational education, its offering learners range opportunities. As times change, teaching methods are evolving and are no longer limited to single format. Everyone has the right to education and learning, but unfortunately, not all students possess the same learning abilities, in such circumstances, the emergence of inclusive learning design enables the creation of tailored approaches to accommodate students with diverse needs (CAST, 2024; Jwad et al., 2022). 
Observed during the hairdressing Certificate III at Box Hill Institute, many students have only a vague understanding of the units relating to haircutting, particularly Combination Haircut Structures (SHBHCUT006), this unit summarises all hair structures and comprises Design Haircut Structure (SHBHCUT001), Solid Form/One Length (SHBHCUT002), Create Graduated Haircut Structures (SHBHCUT003), and Create Layered Hair Cut Structures (SHBHCUT004). 
It is impossible to expect every student to fully understand and apply all these different structures and meet the required standards within a short period of time (12 – 15 months); therefore, inclusive learning design is indispensable in hairdressing education. This paper will focus on how inclusive learning design can be used to support students studying in hairdressing.

When Students Start to get lost
Educators need to employ effective method to inspire students in an inclusive learning environment and help them discover the purpose of learning, rather than merely imitating others blindly (Billett, 2013). Returning to the example mentioned in the introduction, the Combination Haircut Structures (SHBHCUT006), the challenge facing students is not merely to combine several different projection angles. But to understand the changes in shape that occur between 0 to 180 degrees, and this is reflected in the actual haircutting process.
In my delivery and assessment observation, students can easily master Solid Form/One Length (SHBHCUT002) in both zero projection and natural distribution and put into practice through the rules of 7 cutting procedures. But it was only when we got to the Create Graduated haircut Structure (SHBHCUT003) that the real problem, began to emerge. The structure of the graduated shape (Triangle) involves more than simply changing the horizontal parting to diagonal parting; it also includes perpendicular distribution and projections ranging 1 to 90 degrees, the cognitive overload is enough to put students off continuing their learning, if without inclusive learning design support (CESE, 2017; Chandler & Sweller, 1991).
The Centre for Education Statistic and Evaluation (CESE, 2017) notes that working memory is limited when students are processing new or complex information. Chandler and Sweller (1991) also demonstrated that teaching methods can increase or reduce unnecessary cognitive overload. During the haircut, these stresses extend beyond the cognitive realm, issue such as the size of section, the control of the scissors, perception of the guideline all serve to distract the student. I do not believe some students lack of talent or are lazy. Most students start off practicing hard, but when the results fall short of expectations, their concentration drop rapidly. This leads to poor learning outcomes and maybe even cause them to consider giving up.
Although CESE (2017) and Chandler & Sweller (1991) explained why students get cognitive overload, but both do not clarify how students acquire practical control. However, Billett (2013) offers a different perspective, he examines demonstration, instruction and scaffolding methods, and points out that certain types of practice-based knowledge cannot be acquired through independent discovery alone (pp. 149-150). In the industry, students are unable to master haircutting techniques through short videos or professional demonstration, they require clear guidance and master the skills through repeated practice and teacher advice (Kirschner et al., 2006).
Based on the above arguments, educators should reevaluate their approach and draw on their experience in the field to design a more effective learning process, rather than adopting a ‘one-size-fits-all’ teaching method.
